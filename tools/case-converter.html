The Cases Explained

UPPER CASE turns every letter into a capital: "SALE ENDS FRIDAY". Best for short labels, warnings and emphasis — long passages in all caps are harder to read and can look like shouting.

lower case turns every letter small: "sale ends friday". Useful for normalising shouted text, cleaning up data and preparing text you plan to re-case.

Title Case capitalises the first letter of every word: "Sale Ends Friday". The classic choice for headlines, article titles, book titles and video titles.

Sentence case capitalises only the first letter of each sentence, like ordinary writing: "Sale ends Friday." It is the style most websites, apps and documents use for body text and headings today.

aLtErNaTiNg case alternates small and capital letters. It has no formal use — it is a playful style sometimes used in social posts and memes.

Which Case Should You Use?

For headlines, Title Case and Sentence case are both common; many modern style guides prefer Sentence case because it reads more naturally and avoids arguments about which small words to capitalise. For body text, Sentence case is the safe default. Use UPPER CASE sparingly — a word or a short heading, not a paragraph. One honest warning: no converter can recognise proper nouns. Sentence case will lowercase names like "London" or "Ali" mid-sentence, and lower case will flatten acronyms such as "NASA" — so give converted text a quick read and re-capitalise names before you use it.

Fixing Common Case Problems

Typed with Caps Lock on. A whole paragraph in capitals — or worse, inverted case from a stuck Shift key — takes seconds to rescue: convert to lower case first, then to Sentence case, and re-capitalise the names.

Headings copied from PDFs and other documents. Pasted headings often arrive in ALL CAPS or with random capitals. Run them through Title Case or Sentence case so every heading in your document follows one style.

Inconsistent headings across a team. When several people write for one site or channel, headings drift into different styles. Converting every heading with the same setting before publishing keeps titles, menus and video names looking like they belong together.
