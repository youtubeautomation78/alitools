About This Remove Line Breaks Tool

Copy text out of a PDF, an email or an ebook and it usually arrives with a hard line break at the end of every visual line — the paragraph is chopped into dozens of short lines that reflow badly wherever you paste them next. Removing those breaks by hand means pressing Delete at the end of every single line. This free tool to remove line breaks from text online does the whole job in one click: paste your text, press the button, and every line break is replaced with a single space, joining everything into clean, flowing paragraphs.

It works in the toolbox at the top of this page, entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded or stored, there is no sign-up, and the same toolbox can also add line breaks back — one sentence per line — if you ever need the reverse.

How to Use It

  1. Paste your broken text into the toolbox input box at the top of this page.
  2. Click Remove Line Breaks in the Text Tools grid.
  3. Read the joined text in the Output box — breaks have become single spaces and repeated spaces are collapsed.
  4. Click Copy Output and paste the clean paragraph wherever you need it.

Why Does Copied Text Have So Many Line Breaks?

PDFs do not store text as flowing paragraphs. Each line is positioned on the page separately, so when you copy, every visual line ends with a real line-break character. Email clients and ebook readers do something similar when they wrap text at a fixed width. The text looks fine in its original layout, but the moment it lands in a document, a form or a content management system, those fixed breaks fight the new width and produce a ragged, narrow column. Joining the lines restores the paragraph to what it was always meant to be: one block of text that wraps naturally at any width.

When to Remove Line Breaks

  • Cleaning PDF copy — quotes, references and research text pasted into essays and reports.
  • Forwarded emails — messages wrapped at 70-odd characters that need to become readable paragraphs.
  • Forms and spreadsheets — fields that reject multi-line text, or cells that should hold one line.
  • Subtitles and transcripts — caption files where each line is a fragment of a sentence.
  • Web publishing — pasting draft text into a CMS, where stray breaks become unwanted paragraph splits.

Keeping Your Paragraphs

This tool removes every line break, including the blank lines between paragraphs, so a multi-paragraph text becomes one continuous block. If keeping paragraph structure matters, process one paragraph at a time — paste, convert, copy, repeat — or first tidy the text with the Remove Extra Spaces tool on this site, which collapses extra blank lines while keeping one clean break between paragraphs. And if words ever end up joined without a space, it means the original break had no space around it; each break here becomes a space, so check hyphenated line-end words (like "infor-mation") and rejoin those by hand.

Doing It Manually in Word or Excel

If the text already lives in a document, an editor's find and replace can do a slower version of the same job. In Microsoft Word, open Find and Replace (Ctrl+H), search for ^p (the paragraph mark) and replace it with a single space; use ^l for manual line breaks. In Excel, line breaks inside a cell can be found in the Find box by pressing Ctrl+J. These methods work, but they are one document at a time and easy to get wrong on a large file — for anything you have copied from a PDF, email or web page, pasting it here is faster and the original file stays untouched.
