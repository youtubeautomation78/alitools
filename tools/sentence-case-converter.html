About This Sentence Case Converter

Sentence case is the style of ordinary writing: the first word of a sentence starts with a capital letter, and everything else stays lower case except names and the word "I". It is how books, news articles, websites and documents are normally written — which is exactly why text that arrives in the wrong case looks so out of place. A heading pasted from a PDF in ALL CAPS, a paragraph typed with Caps Lock on, a title where Every Word Is Capitalised: this free sentence case converter fixes all of them the moment you paste.

The tool converts live as you type, entirely in your browser. Your text is never uploaded or stored, there is no sign-up, and alongside sentence case you also get UPPER CASE, lower case, Title Case and aLtErNaTiNg case buttons for the times a different style is needed.

How Sentence Case Works

The rules are simple. Capitalise the first letter of the first word in each sentence. Capitalise proper nouns — names of people, places, brands and organisations — and always capitalise the pronoun "I". Everything else stays lower case, including words that would be capitalised in a Title Case headline. This converter automates the mechanical part: it lowercases the whole text, then capitalises the first letter at the start and the first letter after every full stop, exclamation mark or question mark.

That last detail explains the one thing you should check by hand. Because the converter works from punctuation, it cannot tell a name from an ordinary word mid-sentence — "we visited paris in june" becomes "We visited paris in june", and Paris and June need their capitals back. Abbreviations ending in a full stop, such as "Dr.", can also make the next word look like a new sentence. A ten-second scan after converting is all it takes; no automated tool can read meaning well enough to skip it.

Sentence Case or Title Case?

Title Case capitalises every main word of a headline ("The Quick Brown Fox Jumps Over"), while sentence case capitalises only the first ("The quick brown fox jumps over"). Print tradition favours Title Case for book and article titles, but most modern websites, apps and style guides use sentence case for headings and buttons because it reads faster and looks calmer on screen. Whichever your publication uses, consistency matters most — convert every heading with the same tool and setting so they all match.
