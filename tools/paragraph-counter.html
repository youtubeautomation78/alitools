About This Paragraph Counter

Word counts tell you how much you have written; paragraph counts tell you how it is built. Long walls of text lose readers, while short, well-paced paragraphs keep them moving — but structure is hard to judge by eye in a long document. This free paragraph counter online analyses your text as you type: paragraphs, sentences, words and characters, plus the two averages that reveal your pacing at a glance — words per paragraph and words per sentence.

Paste an essay, article or story and the numbers update live. Everything is calculated in your browser, so unpublished drafts and client documents never leave your device, and there is no sign-up or document limit.

How the Counting Works

A paragraph here means a block of text separated from the next by a blank line — the standard convention in typed documents and web writing. Pressing Enter once to start a new line does not begin a new paragraph; leaving an empty line does. Sentences are counted by their ending punctuation — full stops, question marks and exclamation marks — which is accurate for ordinary prose, with one honest caveat: abbreviations such as "Dr." and decimal numbers such as "3.5" contain full stops that can nudge the sentence count. Treat the figures as precise structure statistics for normal writing, not as a linguistic analysis.

Paragraph Length: The Benchmarks That Matter

On screens, paragraphs of roughly 50 to 150 words are comfortable: long enough to develop one idea, short enough to scan. Academic writing traditionally runs longer — 100 to 200 words is common in essays — while web articles and news writing often use paragraphs of just one to three sentences so pages stay readable on phones. Sentence length matters just as much: an average of 15 to 20 words per sentence keeps readers engaged, and averages creeping past 25 usually mean some sentences should be split. Use the two averages on this page together — a document can have a healthy paragraph count and still exhaust readers if every paragraph is one endless sentence.

Why Writers Count Paragraphs

  • Readability checks — spot the 300-word monster paragraph hiding in chapter three.
  • Essay structure — confirm an assignment has the introduction, body and conclusion paragraphs the brief expects.
  • Editing passes — balance a document so dense sections are broken up with breathing room.
  • Web and email writing — adapt print-length paragraphs into the shorter blocks screens demand.
  • Comparing drafts — see at a glance whether a rewrite tightened the structure or just shortened the words.

Paragraph Counter or Word Counter — Which Do You Need?

The two tools answer different questions. If a brief says "800 words", the Word Counter on this site is your page — it adds reading time, readability scores and keyword density. If the brief is about shape rather than size — an essay that must have five body paragraphs, an article that feels dense, a chapter that drags — this page is the right one, because it isolates the structure: how many paragraphs, how long they run, and how your sentences pace inside them. Many writers use both: structure first with the paragraph counts, then length and polish with the word counts.
