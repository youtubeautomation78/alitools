About This Text to Slug Generator

A slug is the readable part of a web address that identifies a page: in example.com/blog/how-to-bake-bread, the slug is "how-to-bake-bread". Good slugs tell visitors and search engines what a page is about before it loads; bad ones are strings of numbers, dates and symbols nobody can read or share. This free slug generator online turns any title or phrase into a clean URL slug as you type — lowercased, stripped of punctuation, and joined with hyphens or underscores.

It also handles the fiddly details automatically: accents are removed (so "Café Guide" becomes "cafe-guide"), "&" is spelled out as "and", repeated separators are collapsed, and stray separators at the start and end are trimmed. You choose whether numbers stay or go, and the character count of your slug is shown live. Everything happens in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

What Makes a Good Slug?

  • Short and descriptive. Use the few words that name the topic — "best-running-shoes" beats "the-very-best-running-shoes-for-beginners-2026". Short slugs are easier to read, share and fit on screen.
  • Keywords, naturally. Include the phrase someone would search for, once. Slugs stuffed with repeated keywords look spammy and help nobody.
  • Hyphens, not underscores. Google's URL guidelines recommend hyphens to separate words in URLs, because an underscore can be read as joining the words into one.
  • Lowercase only. Some servers treat capitalised URLs as different pages, which can split traffic and cause duplicates — another reason this generator lowercases everything.
  • No dates you will regret. A year in a slug ages the page. If the content gets updated yearly, keep the year out (or in the title only) so the address never looks stale.
  • One slug per idea. If two pages would end up with nearly identical slugs, they are probably competing for the same topic — consider merging them into one stronger page instead.

One Rule After Publishing: Do Not Change It Casually

A slug is part of a page's identity. Once a page is published, linked and indexed, changing its slug changes its address — existing links, bookmarks and search results will point at the old one. If you must change a slug, set up a permanent (301) redirect from the old address to the new one so visitors and search engines land in the right place. The cheapest slug strategy is simply to choose well at publishing time, which is what this generator is for: paste the title, check the result reads cleanly and says what the page is about, and only then publish.

Worked Examples

Titles go in one end and slugs come out the other, like this:

  • "10 Tips for Better Pancakes!" → 10-tips-for-better-pancakes (with Keep numbers ticked)
  • "Café & Bakery — Our Menu" → cafe-and-bakery-our-menu (accent removed, "&" spelled out as "and")
  • "How Do I Stop My Dog Barking at Night?" → how-do-i-stop-my-dog-barking-at-night — correct but long; trimming it to stop-dog-barking-at-night keeps the meaning and is easier to share.

That last example is the real skill in slug writing: the generator gives you the faithful conversion, and your judgement decides which filler words the address can live without.
