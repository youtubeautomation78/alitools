About This Character Counter

A character counter tells you exactly how long a piece of text is, measured in individual characters rather than words. That distinction matters more than most people expect: a post on X is capped at 280 characters, an Instagram bio at 150, and Google typically shows only about 50–60 characters of a page title and around 155 characters of a meta description before cutting them off. Going over by even a few characters can truncate a headline, break a form's validation or leave a post unpublishable.

This free character counter online shows four numbers at once: total characters (including spaces and line breaks), characters without spaces, words and lines. It updates with every keystroke, and because the counting happens in your browser, private drafts, client copy and unreleased marketing text never leave your device. There is nothing to install and no account to create.

Platforms change their limits from time to time, and search engines truncate by pixel width rather than a fixed count, so treat these as practical guides and check the platform itself if the exact number matters. Two details catch people out: on X, every link counts as 23 characters because the platform shortens URLs automatically, and a single SMS only holds 160 characters when it uses the basic GSM alphabet — add an emoji or a special character and the segment size drops to 70.

Why Counts Differ Between Tools

If this counter disagrees with your word processor by a character or two, the reason is usually one of three things. Line breaks can be stored as one character or two (the Windows CRLF style). Emoji and many symbols are stored as two or more code units, so one visible emoji may add 2 to a technical count. And some tools quietly exclude trailing spaces or the final line break. None of these is "wrong" — they are different counting rules — so when a platform enforces a hard limit, the safest check is the platform's own counter at posting time.

Tips for Fitting a Character Limit

When a draft is slightly over, small edits usually fix it without changing the meaning. Shorten phrases before cutting ideas: "in order to" becomes "to", "due to the fact that" becomes "because". Remove double spaces after full stops, trim hashtags and mentions that add nothing, and move extra detail into a follow-up post or a link. Count again after each edit — the live counter makes this a quick loop. And leave a small safety margin of a few characters, because the platform may count links, mentions or emoji differently from the draft you checked.
