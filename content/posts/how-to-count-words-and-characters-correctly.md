Whether you are meeting an essay requirement or fitting a caption into a limit, a wrong count can cost you marks or get your text cut off. Here is how counting actually works, and how to get a number you can trust.

## How a word count is calculated

Most tools count a word as any group of characters separated by whitespace. That means "well-known" is one word, while "well known" is two. Numbers, abbreviations and URLs also count as single words. Because tools treat hyphens, dashes and symbols slightly differently, two counters can disagree by a few words on the same text.

## Characters with spaces vs without spaces

The **character count with spaces** includes every letter, number, punctuation mark, space and line break. The **count without spaces** removes the spaces. Assignments usually say which one they want, so check the instructions before you submit. Our [Character Counter](../tools/character-counter.html) shows both numbers live as you type.

## Quick ways to check your count

- **Microsoft Word:** the word count appears in the status bar at the bottom, and Review → Word Count shows characters with and without spaces.
- **Google Docs:** use Tools → Word count, or press Ctrl+Shift+C (Cmd+Shift+C on Mac).
- **Online:** paste your text into the [Word Counter](../tools/word-counter.html) to see words, characters, sentences, paragraphs and reading time at once.

## Why counts differ between tools

- Line breaks may count as one or two characters.
- Emoji and some symbols can count as more than one character.
- Some tools ignore footnotes, headers or text boxes, while others include them.

If a strict limit matters, test your text with the counter that the platform or teacher uses.

## Tips for hitting a target length

1. Write first and count afterward, so the count does not interrupt your thinking.
2. Cut filler words like "very", "really" and "in order to" when you are over the limit.
3. When you are under, add examples or evidence rather than padding.
4. Re-check the count after your final edit, because small changes add up.

You can also use the [Paragraph Counter](../tools/paragraph-counter.html) to see whether your paragraphs are balanced.
